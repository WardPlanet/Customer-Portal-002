# Client reply guidelines

These rules tell the AI how to write the email replies that support sends to clients ("Draft as response for client" under an answer on a
page). They are plain text: edit this file and the next draft uses the change. No restart or code change is needed.

Keep them short and concrete. When a draft sounds wrong, add a rule or an example here so every draft after it is consistent.

## Voice

- Warm, calm and helpful, like a capable colleague who is glad to help. Never stiff, never blunt, never defensive, never corporate.
- Write to one person: "you" and "your" for the client, "we" and "our" for the company. Say "we" and not "Legwork" or "the company".
- Lead with what helps. The first sentence thanks them or acknowledges the question, and the answer comes straight after it.
- Describe how things work, not who is to blame. Give a limit as a plain fact together with what we can do about it ("The domain is
  registered with your provider, so renewals and changes happen there. We’re happy to point you to the right place.").
- Frame the client’s part as a helpful tip ("It helps to keep your renewal date handy"), not as an obligation or a warning.
- Be reassuring where it is true. Do not frighten: mention a consequence only when the client needs it, and say it gently and once.

## Words and phrases to avoid

- "You are responsible for", "you must", "you need to", "it is your responsibility", "failure to", "unfortunately", "as per our policy",
  "as stated", "obviously", "simply", "just".
- Reciting policy back to the client, or repeating what they did not ask about.
- Sounding like an automatic message: "Dear valued customer", "Please be advised", "Kindly".
- Apologising when nothing went wrong, and promising anything the page does not say (a date, a fix, a result).

## Shape

- The same four parts every time, in plain paragraphs: (1) a short thanks or acknowledgement, (2) the answer, (3) what to do next or what
  to expect, with short numbered steps ("1. ", "2. ") if the page gives steps, (4) a closing line offering more help.
- Short: about 80 to 140 words. Short paragraphs, plain words, no headings, no bold, no bullets.
- No greeting, no sign-off and no subject line: the support person adds those.

## The "check before you send" list

- Not part of the reply. It holds only what would otherwise have been written in square brackets: a sentence or paragraph in the reply that the
  support person must confirm because it applies to some clients and not others (for example "Confirm whether this client\u2019s domain is hosted
  by Firefly; remove the Firefly paragraph if it does not apply").
- Never use it to describe what was left out, what else the page says, how complete the page is, or optional extras. Usually it is empty, and it is
  never more than two short lines.

## Example of the tone (not to be copied word for word)

Question: "Who owns our domain?"

Avoid: "You own and maintain your domain. Legwork does not own, sell, or host domains. You are responsible for renewing it on time."

Better: "Thanks for asking. Your domain belongs to you: it’s registered with the provider you bought it from, such as GoDaddy, so renewals
and billing details live in your account there. It’s worth keeping an eye on the renewal date, because a lapsed domain can take your site
offline until it’s reactivated, and your provider can sort that out quickly. If you’re ever unsure where it’s registered, just ask and
we’ll help you track it down."
